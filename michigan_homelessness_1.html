Root Causes

Why Homelessness Happens

Homelessness is rarely the result of a single event. It emerges from the intersection of economic hardship, systemic gaps, and a lack of affordable housing supply.

🏠

Affordable Housing Shortage

Michigan needs an estimated 200,000+ additional affordable housing units. Developers favor higher-margin market-rate construction, leaving low-income renters without options as vacancy rates fall below 3% in major metros.

💸

Wage Stagnation & Poverty

Michigan's minimum wage ($10.33/hr in 2024) requires workers to hold 2–3 jobs to afford average rent. Manufacturing job losses have devastated communities, particularly in Detroit, Flint, and Pontiac.

🏥

Mental Health & Substance Use

Approximately 30% of Michigan's homeless population lives with serious mental illness. Inadequate community mental health infrastructure — following deinstitutionalization — has pushed vulnerable individuals onto the streets.

⚖️

Eviction & Legal Instability

Michigan filed over 80,000 eviction cases in 2023. Detroit is among the top 10 U.S. cities for eviction rates. Without right-to-counsel programs, most tenants face landlords' attorneys alone.

🔗

Systemic Discrimination

Black Michiganders represent 14% of the population but 40% of the homeless population — a direct legacy of redlining, discriminatory lending, and unequal enforcement of housing codes.

🎖️

Veteran & Youth Homelessness

Over 700 veterans experience homelessness in Michigan on any given night. Youth aging out of foster care face high homelessness risk — 40% experience homelessness within 2 years of leaving the system.

Solutions

Policy Pathways Forward

Researchers, advocates, and housing experts broadly agree on evidence-based interventions that have reduced homelessness in peer states.

POLICY 01

Housing First Programs

Provide permanent housing without preconditions like sobriety or employment. Studies show Housing First reduces chronic homelessness by up to 88% and is more cost-effective than emergency shelter.

Proven 88% reduction in chronic homelessness
POLICY 02

Inclusionary Zoning Reform

Require new residential developments to include 10–20% affordable units. Michigan's preemption law currently limits local governments' ability to mandate affordable housing — this must be repealed.

Creates sustainable affordable unit pipeline
POLICY 03

Emergency Rental Assistance

Permanent state-funded ERA programs prevent evictions before they become homelessness. COVID-era programs proved the model: $2.5B in federal ERA funds kept 200,000+ Michigan households stably housed in 2021–2022.

Prevents downstream emergency shelter costs
POLICY 04

Right to Counsel — Evictions

Guarantee legal representation for low-income tenants in eviction proceedings. New York City's program reduced evictions by 40% and saved $320M annually in shelter and social service costs.

40% reduction in evictions where implemented
POLICY 05

Behavioral Health Integration

Co-locate mental health, substance use, and housing services under one roof. Michigan's community mental health system needs $300M+ in new investment to meet current demand. Assertive Community Treatment (ACT) teams show strong outcomes.

60% reduction in hospitalizations among participants
POLICY 06

Land Trust & Social Housing Models

Community Land Trusts permanently remove housing from speculative markets. The Detroit Community Land Trust is a local model. Social housing — publicly financed, mixed-income developments — expands this approach to scale.

Permanently affordable; protects community wealth
Timeline

Michigan Housing Policy: Key Moments

1968
Fair Housing Act Passed

Federal law bans housing discrimination by race, religion, and national origin. Michigan's legacy of redlining continues to shape housing inequality today.

1999
Michigan Preempts Local Rent Control

State law strips cities' authority to enact rent stabilization ordinances, limiting local responses to housing cost spikes.

2013
Detroit Files for Bankruptcy

60,000 blighted structures, 78,000 abandoned parcels, and a 25% poverty rate create entrenched housing instability in Michigan's largest city.

2015–2019
Flint Water Crisis

Lead contamination displaces residents, devalues properties, and deepens housing insecurity in an already vulnerable community.

2021
$2.5B Federal ERA Funding

COVID emergency rental assistance prevents mass evictions. Michigan distributes funds faster than most states, a model for future programs.

2023–2025
Governor Whitmer's Housing Plan

$100M+ in state investments target affordable housing production, homelessness prevention, and community land trusts. Progress is ongoing.
